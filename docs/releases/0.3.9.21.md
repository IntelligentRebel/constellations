## What's new in 0.3.9.21: a bug hunt through the whole code

The whole code was read again, area by area (combat, movement, economy, play by e-mail, diplomacy, events, computer players, interface), then put to the test: 40 games between computer players of 100 to 180 years in every configuration (galaxy shapes, sizes, races, rules switched off), with the invariants checked every turn (fuel, holds, populations, minerals) and every game compared with its replayed copy, plus 6,000 random actions on the real interface, in French and in English. Over a hundred defects fixed. The main ones:

**A turn could depend on the game played before it.** The map of closed borders was cached under a key that ignored the position and radius of the culture sources: a loaded game and the same game played on could compute different turns. The same flaw, less serious, affected the cloud routing grid, the cloud layer of the map and the computers' estimate of hulls.

**Combat**
- Every empire at a battle was credited with everyone else's losses, allies and bystanders included (Hunter objective, achievements, reports); destroyed convoys went to every armed empire present. Each empire now gets what it destroyed.
- An empire learned the technology of its allies' ships and studied their wrecks; a fleet at peace with everyone gained experience from a battle it only watched.
- The damage of flares, anomalies and cataclysms was pooled: it destroyed whole ships that still had armor, and left fuel and cargo beyond the tanks.
- The Electronic warfare doctrine cloaked the design sheet, not the fleet the scanners look for. The Regression bomb counted as an upgrade of ordinary bombs. Capacitors counted twice in the damage per round shown.
- A station whose default plan aimed at nothing never opened fire, although it fights aiming at armed ships.
- Upgrading a design forgot the operations building it, the refit orders aiming at it and its "scout" box. Refitting to a smaller hold destroyed the cargo: it now stays on the world.
- The combat simulator wrote some forty fake pages in the chronicle at every click.

**Movement and fuel**
- A typed speed was not bounded: a patrol at Warp 7.5 flew without fuel, a packet at Warp -16 never decayed.
- A jump refused by a gate dumped the cargo and then left the fleet stuck; a repeating route stopped after a jump; a mixed ramscoop fleet believed it was dry; a detour after a wormhole started from its entrance.
- Route estimates forgot the fuel top-up and what fuel transports make: they announced breakdowns that never came. Year-end positions were a year late after a waypoint crossed exactly at year end (interceptions and tankers off).
- Fleets stayed trapped in the hidden cluster; splitting or giving a fleet kept its standing orders.

**Economy**
- Opening a world's production queue could trigger a free golden age (patronage was simulated for real).
- An abandoned production line, or an emptied queue, lost the minerals already committed; automatic alchemy destroyed the remainder; a gap between terraforming paid and terraforming done made you pay forever.
- The golden age lasted 9 years instead of 10, disorganisation 4 instead of 5; the Generalized Research splash fell on the wrong field after a level; Orbital Life colonies bought a factory and a mine; a paid megaproject was abandoned; deleting a queue template crashed production.
- A governor's patronage bypassed the Biotechnology level required; the homeworld's concentration floor did nothing.

**Play by e-mail**
- Merging orders replaced whole planets: a tribute, a gift or cargo unloaded at someone else's created or destroyed minerals. It now works by differences: a second orders file from the same player undoes what the first one did, a turn file made after other merges does not count them twice, and two players loading the same minerals no longer double them (checked on 5,500 random merges).
- Wrecks, troop landings, borders a computer had opened and its grudges were lost; a second turn file reused ids (a ship vanished); a player could tick rules and crash the host; two empires with non-Latin names got the same file name; a damaged file crashed the import.

**Diplomacy, espionage, events**
- Computers learned nothing from their spies: the coalition against the leading player never formed.
- Cancelling your treaties just before declaring war avoided the betrayal; an ally of the hegemon ganged up on it; an offer from a dead empire could still be accepted; spies beyond the maximum were paid for nothing; minerals offered by an empire with no world were counted but never paid.
- An anomaly decision or a dilemma hit a world that had passed to a rival; a probe revealed hidden stars and the far end of a sealed wormhole; the climate engine overshot its target; the nemesis capital clause could never trigger; a world emptied by the Reapers stayed a regional capital, with no owner.
- Several screens showed what your scanners do not see: a guardian's real strength, the empire that took a site, the position of pirate raids, the Breach waves and the fleets defending it. They now show your measurements and what your informants announce; a raid or a wave changing target is announced.

**Computer players**
- A computer could put at the head of a queue something it could not pay for and block a world for decades; an empty ark tried to colonize forever; fleets left without the fuel to come back and stayed stranded; the defense against an approaching fleet never saw its course.

**Interface**
- A cargo dialog left open over a turn (or after an undo) moved cargo across any distance.
- After loading, the commanded fleet could belong to another empire; an undo was not saved; deleting a save could restore it from the backup copy.
- The race wizard accepted 7.01 and gained 800 points that way; an invalid imported race broke the new game screen for good; research per year counted Generalized Research twice and ignored the bonuses.
- The help texts gave maneuvering jets four times their combat speed, and were wrong about smart and regression bombs and the wandering black hole.
- Mission buttons took a fleet off its standing order (remote mining, patrol, refuelling) or off the study of an anomaly; "Send" went even when a ship was already on its way.
- And dozens of details: "Infinity years", "null years", the CSV export, the music catching up on missed chords all at once, a pinch moving a waypoint, "Go here" aiming at the centre of a minefield, the tutorial ignoring "Build and send".
