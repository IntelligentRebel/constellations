## What's new in 0.3.9.19: the common enemy

When an empire runs away with the game, computer or player, the others now draw together against it, in proportion to its lead over the second one (nothing at 1.2 times its score, everything at 2.5 times, fully from 2430 on, since the first scores mean little), judged from the scores each empire knows.

- **Their opinion of one another rises** with that lead (up to +50), even between empires that dislike each other, except toward the one a computer has sworn to destroy and toward the dominant empire's allies; their opinion of the dominant empire drops (up to −40).
- **Alliances against it:** they need the opinion of a common enemy at war (30 instead of 50). The computers offer them before any other treaty, to players too ("an alliance against the X, whose lead threatens the whole galaxy"), and the chronicle tells against whom it was signed.
- **War on it:** they declare it more readily, even when already at war elsewhere, and when one attacks it, its computer allies who fear it as much follow (a player ally is told, free to follow or not).
- The Diplomacy page tells when such dominance begins.
- Every level does it, except that an easy computer never gangs up on a player (it allies with you against a dominant computer). Before, only Hard and Expert did, and barely.

Measured over 6 games of 150 years (6 Hard computers, the same map with and without it): in the three where one empire pulled away, its final lead over the second fell from 2.36 to 1.52 times, from 1.60 to 1.32 and from 1.23 to 1.13, with 3 to 15 alliances signed against it; in the other three, nothing changes.
