## What's new in 0.3.9.22: a second bug hunt, and a faster engine

The whole code was read again by sixteen reviewers, area by area, and the fixes were reviewed from outside by another AI (Codex). Then the usual tests: every regression check of the earlier versions, a new one for this version, games between computer players, and random actions on the interface.

**Faster.** A turn of a late game takes about 25 to 30 % less time: the specs of ship designs, races and terraforming are remembered instead of recomputed thousands of times a year, path finding around the clouds reuses its buffers and its last results, and the scanners are indexed by position. The games are identical to the bit: the same save gives the same turns as before.

**A turn could depend on what the interface had looked at.** Looking at a rival's design (battle report, designer, intelligence) stored a spec computed without the hidden shifts of the tech tree, and the next turn used it (costs, salvage). Fixed, and checked on many designs.

**Engine**
- A route left with only its detour points (its destination deleted) stopped the generation of the turn.
- Alchemy was paid for and its minerals never arrived; colonists over the maximum produced at 150 % instead of half rate; the minerals put into a queue were lost when a world changed hands or was emptied.
- A regional capital that could not be founded yet was dropped with what it had cost: it now waits.
- The queue estimate credited two lines of the same kind with each other's builds, and used the present year for every year ahead.
- A jump refused by a stargate cost a year: the fleet now leaves at once at its ideal speed, as the route panel says.
- The resources of ships scrapped by Ultimate Recyclers were wiped before they could be spent.
- Splits and transfers accepted negative, fractional or NaN amounts, and a split could overfill a hold; troops that could not land overfilled the hold of a fleet, or went aboard a fleet given away meanwhile.
- Play by e-mail: minerals taken from a fleet that had already moved them were made twice; the host took a player's id counter and gave its own objects ids in that player's range; a monster woken by a player's orders was ignored by the others.
- Diplomacy: proposals made pointless by a deal stayed in the inbox and expired as refusals, even those of dead empires; the lasting peace restarted with each alliance; computers judged rivals on their real scores, and the coalition pushed an ally of the leader to turn on it.

**Computer players**
- They chose colonies by territories they had not seen, and an ark refused there tried again every year; bombers alone held useless sieges under stations; freighters abroad never came home in peace; converted arks stopped the building of new ones; mine layers and arks kept claims they no longer served; a rival's Portail was ignored while their own was further along.
- One-click missions flew through closed borders and declared war.

**Interface**
- The end of the game is now shown to every human player, in hot seat and by turn files.
- Several pages named empires never met or showed values your spies cannot know (intelligence lines, culture, lairs, anomalies, rival designs); orders could go to a fleet given away; dragging a waypoint could give the order to another fleet.
- Help texts corrected: race factors on armour, shields and scanners, the built-in scanner (Jack of all trades only), the Orbital Fort, the smart bomb floor, the energy dampener, the half-point manoeuvre bonus; the tutorial cited a queue item that does not exist.

The reviews of threats, discoveries and movement reported more issues, kept for the next version.
